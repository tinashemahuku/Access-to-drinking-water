# Global Access to Safe Drinking Water

A two-part data analysis project investigating global access to safe and
affordable drinking water: what access looks like today, and how it has
changed over time.

- **[Part 1: Distribution & Predictors](part1/)**: How water-service
  levels vary across countries, income groups, and urban/rural
  populations, using a cross-sectional snapshot of ~200 countries.
- **[Part 2: Rate of Change (2000–2020)](part2/)**: How fast countries,
  and rural vs. urban populations within them, are gaining access over
  time.

Together, the two parts move from **"where do things stand"** to **"how
fast is it changing, and for whom."**

## Highlights

- The dataset covers **99.6% of the world's population**, so conclusions
  generalize well at a global level.
- **Income group is the strongest predictor** of water access, far more
  than population size or urbanization. High-income countries average
  ~100% basic access; low-income countries average ~63%.
- Basic access is heavily **left-skewed**: most countries are near full
  coverage, but a smaller group of countries pulls the average down
  substantially, especially in rural areas.
- **Rural access is improving faster than urban access** almost
  everywhere (0.48 vs. 0.15 percentage points/year on average). Rural
  areas are catching up, not falling further behind.
- Regions closest to full access (Europe & Central Asia, North America)
  show the slowest rates of change, not because of stagnation, but
  because there's little room left to improve.

## Method

All cleaning, pivoting, and analysis was done in Excel/Google Sheets. The
original workbooks are in the part1 and part2 folders. Every image in this
repository is a direct screenshot from those workbooks. No chart was
recreated or made outside of Excel/Sheets.

## Data source

World Health Organization / UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme (JMP)
estimates of drinking water, sanitation and hygiene access.
